# Tell me about yourself / introduce your background.

## Problem In Simple Words

Opening introduction. The interviewer is checking clarity, seniority, communication, and whether your experience maps to the role.

## Input / Context Meaning

This prompt is checking how you behave in real engineering situations. For a 7+ years profile, answer with a specific story or a precise operating principle, then show judgment, ownership, and measurable learning. For this question, keep the focus on: Tell me about yourself / introduce your background.

## School-Level Intuition

Use present-past-future. For 7+ years, lead with your current engineering identity, then give proof from backend/system work, then connect to the role.

## Basic Answer

I am a backend engineer with 7+ years of experience, mainly around Java/Spring services, APIs, and production workflows. I have worked on features from design to implementation and testing. My strength is taking ambiguous requirements, breaking them into clean services, and communicating tradeoffs clearly.

## Strong 7+ Years Answer

I am a backend engineer with 7+ years of experience building Java/Spring based services, APIs, and production workflows. My strongest areas are turning ambiguous product requirements into clean service boundaries, writing maintainable code, and thinking through reliability from the start. Recently I have worked deeply on ecommerce-style flows like catalog, cart, order, inventory, invoice, and event-driven integration. I enjoy roles where I can own a feature end to end: clarify requirements, design the model, implement APIs, write tests, and explain tradeoffs clearly. Right now I am focused on strengthening system design and interview-style problem solving so I can contribute at a senior backend level.

## Why This Answer Works

This works because it gives the interviewer concrete evidence instead of adjectives. It shows what you personally did, why you chose that action, and how the result changed the project, team, or decision quality.

## Anti-Pattern Answer

Listing every company and year chronologically. That makes the answer long and hides your senior signal.

## Advanced Version / Add-On

At senior level, I would add that I think beyond the endpoint: data model, failure modes, observability, and how the team will maintain the code after release. I would also mention mentoring/reviewing if the role expects senior ownership.

## STAR Breakdown

Situation: Interviewer asks for a concise intro.
Task: Show senior backend fit quickly.
Action: Present current identity, proof from backend systems, and target role.
Result: Interviewer gets a clear map of your experience and where to probe next.

## Dry Run / Walkthrough

Present: 7+ years backend engineer. Past proof: built service workflows and production APIs. Future: looking for senior backend role where design, reliability, and ownership matter.

## Follow-Up Questions To Prepare

- If asked “why now,” say: I am looking for a role where I can combine backend implementation with design ownership and reliable delivery.
- If asked “why this company,” connect your backend/system strengths to their product domain.
- If asked “biggest strength,” use clarity under ambiguity plus maintainable implementation.

## Final Interview Checklist

- Start with the situation in one sentence.
- Make your personal action clear.
- Add a senior signal: tradeoff, risk, metric, mentoring, or production impact.
- Keep the story under two minutes, then let the interviewer ask deeper probes.
- Avoid blame; show ownership and learning.
