# How do you handle not knowing the answer to a technical question?

## Problem In Simple Words

Meta interview prompt. They want humility and structured problem solving.

## Input / Context Meaning

This prompt is checking how you behave in real engineering situations. For a 7+ years profile, answer with a specific story or a precise operating principle, then show judgment, ownership, and measurable learning. For this question, keep the focus on: How do you handle not knowing the answer to a technical question?

## School-Level Intuition

Show that you do not bluff. Clarify, reduce the problem, reason from fundamentals, and communicate.

## Basic Answer

If I do not know something, I do not bluff. I state what I know, clarify the requirement, work through a smaller example, and explain how I would verify the missing detail.

## Strong 7+ Years Answer

If I do not know the exact answer, I first say what I do know and clarify the requirement. Then I try a smaller example and reason from fundamentals. For coding, I may write a brute force first and optimize from there. For design, I state assumptions and compare options. If I am missing a specific API or fact, I say so directly and explain how I would verify it. I think the important thing is to keep the interviewer inside my reasoning instead of going silent or pretending.

## Why This Answer Works

This works because it gives the interviewer concrete evidence instead of adjectives. It shows what you personally did, why you chose that action, and how the result changed the project, team, or decision quality.

## Anti-Pattern Answer

Bluffing or saying “I do not know” and stopping.

## Advanced Version / Add-On

Senior version: distinguish unknown syntax from unknown concept. Syntax can be looked up; uncertain architecture needs assumptions, risk analysis, and validation plan.

## STAR Breakdown

Situation: Asked unfamiliar technical question.
Task: Keep solving transparently.
Action: Clarify, reduce to fundamentals, propose baseline, identify missing fact.
Result: Interviewer sees structured problem solving instead of panic.

## Dry Run / Walkthrough

Unknown API. State behavior needed. Implement with known structure. Mention exact syntax can be checked.

## Follow-Up Questions To Prepare

- If asked “production unknown,” mention rollback/mitigation first.
- If asked “coding unknown,” write brute force.
- If asked “design unknown,” state assumptions.

## Final Interview Checklist

- Start with the situation in one sentence.
- Make your personal action clear.
- Add a senior signal: tradeoff, risk, metric, mentoring, or production impact.
- Keep the story under two minutes, then let the interviewer ask deeper probes.
- Avoid blame; show ownership and learning.
