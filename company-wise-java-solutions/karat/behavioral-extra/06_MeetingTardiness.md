# How would you address a teammate often arriving late to meetings?

## Problem In Simple Words

Interpersonal judgment prompt.

## Input / Context Meaning

This prompt is checking how you behave in real engineering situations. For a 7+ years profile, answer with a specific story or a precise operating principle, then show judgment, ownership, and measurable learning. For this question, keep the focus on: How would you address a teammate often arriving late to meetings?

## School-Level Intuition

Be respectful, private, and impact-focused.

## Basic Answer

I would address repeated meeting tardiness privately. I would describe the pattern, explain the impact, ask if there is a constraint, and agree on a fix such as changing time or using async updates.

## Strong 7+ Years Answer

I would not call them out publicly. I would speak privately and say I noticed the pattern, explain the impact on the team, and ask if there is a scheduling conflict or another issue. If there is a legitimate constraint, I would adjust the meeting time or create an async update path. If not, I would agree on expectations. The goal is to solve the team problem without assuming bad intent.

## Why This Answer Works

This works because it gives the interviewer concrete evidence instead of adjectives. It shows what you personally did, why you chose that action, and how the result changed the project, team, or decision quality.

## Anti-Pattern Answer

Public embarrassment or passive-aggressive comments.

## Advanced Version / Add-On

Senior answer balances empathy and accountability. Do not shame publicly, but do not ignore repeated team impact.

## STAR Breakdown

Situation: Teammate repeatedly late.
Task: Protect team time respectfully.
Action: Private conversation, ask reason, agree fix.
Result: Clear expectation without unnecessary conflict.

## Dry Run / Walkthrough

Observe pattern -> private talk -> ask reason -> agree fix -> follow up.

## Follow-Up Questions To Prepare

- If asked “not manager,” mention peer feedback.
- If asked “continues,” escalate through manager.
- If asked “valid constraint,” adjust process.

## Final Interview Checklist

- Start with the situation in one sentence.
- Make your personal action clear.
- Add a senior signal: tradeoff, risk, metric, mentoring, or production impact.
- Keep the story under two minutes, then let the interviewer ask deeper probes.
- Avoid blame; show ownership and learning.
